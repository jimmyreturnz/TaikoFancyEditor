# Taiko Fancy Arranger v3.3.1

Slow playback and hitsounds, both rebuilt from measurement.

## Changed

- **Hitsounds are mixed directly into the song's audio** instead of played as separate triggered sounds. A note now lands on its own millisecond of the music at every speed — including staying correctly in sync if you change the hitsound offset or edit notes mid-playback — and a fast scroll or seek can no longer stack a section's hitsounds into one loud burst. It isn't guarded against any more; it's structurally impossible.
- **Slow playback (25/50/75%) is substantially more accurate.** The time-stretch grain shortened from 117ms to 20ms, which also sounds better on real tracks (a kick drum used to come apart at 25%) and costs less CPU. Measured worst-case disagreement between the playhead and the music: 45.1ms → 9.7ms at 25%, 30.0ms → 4.2ms at 50%, 13.0ms → 2.8ms at 75%.
- **Music offset (ms) and Hitsound offset (ms) are independent again** — the music's own volume no longer attenuates the hitsounds mixed alongside it.

## Known limitation

At 25% speed, a small (single-digit-millisecond) timing gap between the playhead and the music remains, under investigation. It doesn't fully account for a reported "kick sounds late" observation at that speed on its own; two likely causes were checked directly and ruled out. Notes on what's been tried are in `docs/DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md`.

## Why release this

The hitsound and slow-playback accuracy work stands on its own — measured, tested (41 modules, 1381 tests), and a clear improvement over 3.3.0. The residual offset is real but small and still being chased; not worth holding the rest of this back for.
